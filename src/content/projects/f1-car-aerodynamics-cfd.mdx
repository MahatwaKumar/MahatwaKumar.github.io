## Overview

A modern ground-effect F1 car makes most of its downforce from the floor, not the wings. I wanted to see that for myself in CFD: build a complete car, simulate it at speed, and understand where its downforce, drag and aero balance come from.

The first car I simulated was badly front-loaded: 87% of its downforce sat on the front axle. Finding out why, and fixing it, became the core of the project.

<div class="note">

All CFD numbers on this page are **simulated** on a single coarse mesh. They are trends for a generic car, not validated predictions. The car is my own design, with proportions estimated from reference photos, and uses no team data.

</div>

### Geometry

I modeled the design car in CadQuery as 59 parametric parts: a three-element front wing, nose, survival cell, sidepods, halo, suspension arms, wheels, beam wing and rear wing. For CFD I simplified it and fused everything into **one watertight solid**. I checked every build for a valid single solid, watertightness, zero self-intersecting faces and size limits before it went anywhere near the mesher.

### Domain and boundary conditions

I modeled half the car with a symmetry plane at the centreline, which halves the cell count and let me spend the Ansys Student licence's limit of 1,048,576 cells on resolution where it matters.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Setting | Choice |
|---|---|
| Domain | x from −8.0 to 22.52 m (car 5.52 m long), 5 m wide (half), 5 m tall |
| Inlet | 50 m/s, 1% turbulence intensity, viscosity ratio 10 |
| Outlet | Pressure outlet, 0 Pa gauge |
| Ground | Moving no-slip wall at 50 m/s |
| Wheels | Rotating walls, 142 rad/s (0.352 m rolling radius) |
| Side and top | Symmetry |
| Solver | Pressure-based coupled, steady, k-ω SST |
| Air | ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, μ = 1.7894 × 10⁻⁵ Pa·s |

</div>

### Mesh strategy

I meshed the car with the Fluent Meshing watertight workflow:

- **Surface sizing:** 8 to 60 mm on the car, with 18° curvature refinement and a 3 mm minimum on the wings, where the leading edges and slots are tight.
- **Body-of-influence boxes:** a 160 mm box around the car and wake, and a **20 mm box under the floor** from x = 1.4 to 5.5 m, where the venturi flow accelerates.
- **Boundary layer:** 3 prism layers.
- **Volume:** poly-hexcore, **781,527 cells** for the v4 car, with a minimum orthogonal quality of **0.150**. The Fluent mesh check passes.

### Floor redesign (v4)

The redesigned floor has two venturi tunnels with a throat at X = 3.3 m and an ~8° diffuser. I defined the tunnel roof height, keel and width at stations along the car.

## Results

The redesigned floor nearly doubled simulated downforce while drag stayed almost the same.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity (full car, 50 m/s, mean of last 100 iterations) | Run 1: placeholder floor, fixed wheels | v4: venturi floor, rotating wheels |
|---|---|---|
| Downforce | 2,541 N | **4,909 N** |
| Drag | 1,819 N | **1,833 N** |
| CdA / ClA | 1.19 / 1.66 m² | **1.20 / 3.21 m²** |
| Lift-to-drag ratio | 1.40 | **2.68** |
| Front share of downforce | 87% | **62%** |
| Mesh cells (half car) | 839,517 | 781,527 |

</div>

### Why the first car was front-heavy

1. The first car put 87% of its downforce on the front axle.
2. I first suspected the mesh, so I added the 20 mm under-floor refinement. It barely changed the balance, so the mesh was not the cause.
3. I then broke the force down by component, integrating the exported surface pressure and shear over each part. The front wing made 2,435 N, while the placeholder floor made only 976 N.
4. My redesigned venturi floor makes **3,012 N**, with under-floor Cp reaching about −0.85 at the throat.
5. The balance is still front-heavy compared with a real car (about 40–45% front), because the front wing (2,469 N) acts 0.93 m ahead of the front axle.

The v4 values fall inside rough public ranges for 2022–23 F1 cars: CdA 1.0–1.5 m², ClA 3–5 m² and L/D about 2.5–4. I did not tune anything to hit them, and being in range does not validate the CFD.

## How I checked the results

- **Convergence.** I stopped each solve on force flatness, not residuals: forces had to be flat to 1% over a 100-iteration window (checked every 50 iterations, minimum 300). On v4, drag varied 0.86% over the last 100 iterations, which **meets** the target. Downforce varied 1.98%, which does **not**, so I report v4 as the mean of the last 100 iterations.
- **Force breakdown.** On the v3 run, my surface integration of pressure and shear reproduced Fluent's own force totals, so I trusted the per-component numbers.
- **Mesh quality.** The Fluent mesh check passes, with a minimum orthogonal quality of 0.150 on v4 (0.103 on run 1).
- **Units.** I checked the mesh extents before every solve, so a scale error between CAD (mm) and solver (m) could not slip through.
- **CAD.** Every build was confirmed valid, a single solid, watertight and free of self-intersections.
- **Not done:** a mesh-independence study (one mesh per design, so the discretisation error is unknown) and any experimental validation.

## Challenges & what I learned

- **Thin trailing edges broke the surface mesh.** The wings' sharp trailing edges and narrow slots would not mesh cleanly, so I blunted the trailing edges to 10 mm and widened the slots. That made the wings meshable, but it changes their load, and I list it as a limitation.
- **Refining the mesh did not fix the balance.** I expected the 87%-front balance to be a resolution problem. When the 20 mm under-floor box barely moved it, I learned to break the forces down by component before changing anything else. That breakdown showed the floor was the problem.
- **The cell budget shapes every decision.** With a hard limit of 1,048,576 cells, I used a half-car symmetry model and put refinement boxes only where the physics needed them: under the floor and around the wings.
- **A wake test failed its own gate.** I tried using this car's CFD wake as the inlet for a following car, to replace the fitted wake in my [dirty-air lap-time model](/projects/f1-dirty-air-lap-time-model/). The follower *gained* downforce, which failed the gate I set in advance, so I did not change the model.

## Limitations & next steps

**Limitations**
- Simulated, coarse trends, not validated predictions: one mesh, no mesh-independence study, only 3 prism layers, and steady RANS on a flow with unsteady shedding.
- The car is generic: photo-estimated proportions, simplified suspension, plain cylindrical tyres without rims, and a simple floor shape.
- The blunted 10 mm trailing edges and wider slots change the wing loads.
- A half-car symmetry model cannot show yaw or side-to-side shedding.
- I started a wing set-up trim to move the balance further rearward, but stopped it before convergence, so I report no result.

**Next steps (planned, not done)**
- A three-mesh grid study with GCI.
- Restoring thin trailing edges with local refinement.
- A short unsteady (URANS) check.
- A speed sweep at 50, 70 and 90 m/s.
