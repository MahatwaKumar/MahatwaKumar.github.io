## Overview

Racing wings make downforce, and they bend under it. The FIA limits the bending with static tests: a load pad pushes on one spot of the wing, and the deflection must stay under a limit.

Real airflow pushes on the whole surface at once, with a different size and shape of load, so a wing can pass the static test and still behave differently on track. I built a model that predicts deflection from the real aerodynamic load, designed a physical test to check it, and used it to answer how flex changes downforce.

**Status:** the model, the test design and the print files are complete. The physical tests have not been run, so every result here is a **prediction**, made with placeholder material stiffness.

### Test article and scaling

The flap uses the NASA LS(1)-0413MOD section with a 100 mm chord and a 210 mm span between mounts (a 230 mm printed part with two 10 mm end blocks). It runs inverted at 8° incidence, at a ride height of h/c = 0.5, with a design speed of 40 m/s.

The scale factor is 0.4, assuming a 0.25 m full-size flap chord. At equal dynamic pressure, force scales with s² and deflection with s. So the FIA flap test (60 N with a 10 mm limit in the 2026 regulations) becomes **9.6 N with a 4.0 mm limit** for this model.

### Aerodynamics

- **2D:** a Hess-Smith panel method (source panels plus a uniform vortex, with a Kutta condition) and a mirror-image ground plane, tabulated over incidence and ride height.
- **Viscous correction:** from NeuralFoil, giving η ≈ 0.79 at 40 m/s (Re ≈ 2.7 × 10⁵).
- **3D:** a vortex-ring lattice with modelled endplates and ground images, for the spanwise load distribution.

<Chart id="wf-ground" title="Ground effect: downforce vs ride height" caption="Predicted 2D section coefficients (inviscid). Results below h/c ≈ 0.2 are not physical because the model has no stall." />

### Structure

- **Section model:** perimeter walls (3 × 0.45 mm) plus 15% infill at a reduced efficiency. This gives the bending stiffness EI, torsional stiffness GJ (Bredt) and the shear centre of the closed thin-walled section.
- **Beam finite elements:** Euler-Bernoulli bending plus St Venant torsion, 44 elements with 3 DOF per node and consistent nodal loads.
- **Coupling:** bending moves the flap toward the ground and twist changes its incidence; both feed back into the aero load. I also checked static torsional divergence as a generalised eigenproblem, K v = q A v.
- **Uncertainty bands:** ±15% material stiffness, ±20% shear modulus and an infill efficiency from 0 to 0.6.

## Results

All values are **predicted** with placeholder stiffness: typical printed PLA (E = 3.0 GPa) and the CF-nylon datasheet value (E = 4.15 GPa). I will recompute them from measured coupon values before the bench test.

|---|---|---|
| Downforce at 40 m/s, 3D with viscous correction | 22.36 N (CL 1.09) | 22.36 N |
| Trailing-edge deflection, scaled FIA test (9.6 N) | 0.287 mm (band 0.224–0.386) | 0.207 mm (band 0.162–0.279) |

| Trailing-edge deflection under aero load, 40 m/s | 0.235 mm | 0.170 mm |
| Trailing-edge deflection under aero load, 80 m/s | 1.005 mm | 0.726 mm |
| Flex effect on downforce, 40 / 80 m/s | +0.069% / +0.29% | +0.050% / +0.21% |
| Bending / torsional stiffness EI / GJ | 15.0 / 17.2 N·m² | 20.8 / 23.8 N·m² |
| Shear centre / centre of pressure | 0.281c / 0.331c | same |
| Static torsional divergence | none | none |

**What the predictions say**

1. **The air load is larger but bends the flap less.** At 40 m/s the predicted aero load (22.4 N) is about 2.3 times the scaled FIA test load, yet it gives only 0.82 times the trailing-edge deflection. It is spread over the span and centred at 33% chord, near the stiffer leading edge, while the test pad sits near the trailing edge.
2. **Flex adds a little downforce.** Bending moves the flap toward the ground, where ground effect raises downforce. Twist lowers the incidence and takes some back, because the centre of pressure (0.331c) is behind the shear centre (0.281c). The same arrangement rules out torsional divergence.
3. **A printed flap this size is too stiff to be a "flexi-wing".** All 30 wall and infill combinations pass the scaled test by at least 5.3×. A useful flexi-wing would need thinner or longer parts, or a composite layup with bend-twist coupling.

## How I checked the results

34 checks pass, each comparing a part of the model with an exact or known answer:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Check | Tolerance |
|---|---|
| Panel-method lift vs exact Kármán-Trefftz aerofoil (0°, 4°, 8°) | within 1% |
| Panel-method Cp vs exact solution | max error < 0.05 |
| Cusped Joukowski aerofoil, panel refinement | error falls with refinement, < 2% at the finest mesh |
| Ground plane: flow through the ground | normal velocity < 10⁻¹⁰ |
| Ground effect far from the ground vs free air | within 0.5% |
| Panel-count convergence | change < 1% |
| Beam FE: simply supported, cantilever and torsion cases | within 10⁻⁶ of closed form |
| Aero-to-beam load transfer: force, moment, torque | conserved within 0.1% |

## Challenges & what I learned

- **My first load estimate was nine times too high.** The 2D inviscid result suggested about 85 N of aero load, roughly 9× the scaled FIA point load. Adding 3D tip loss (×0.64 even with endplates) and the viscous correction (×0.79) brought it down to about 2.3×, and spreading the load over the span brought the deflection down to 0.82× the FIA test. I learned not to draw conclusions from a 2D upper bound.
- **The span had to fit the printer.** I started with a 400 mm span but cut it to 210 mm between mounts, so the flap prints in one piece on a 250 mm bed. A joint at mid-span would have sat at the peak bending moment.
- **I chose a support the model can trust.** I mounted the flap simply supported on two ridges 220 mm apart: free to rotate in bending, held in twist. That makes the test check my model, not an unknown clamp stiffness.
- **Validation data was hard to get.** I couldn't get Tyrrell 026 aerofoil coordinates or Zerihan & Zhang ground-effect pressure data, so I used the NASA LS(1)-0413MOD section and listed the comparison as still to do.

## Limitations & next steps

- **No physical test data yet.** The coupons, dogbones and wing test are designed and ready to print, but have not been run. Nothing here is measured or validated.
- **Placeholder material stiffness.** Printed parts are often less stiff than datasheets.
- **Inviscid aerodynamics plus a free-air viscous factor.** Suction peaks are over-predicted, there is no stall, and results below h/c ≈ 0.2 are not physical.
- **Scaling assumes a 0.25 m full-size flap chord**, which is not published. I used the 10 mm limit from the 2026 regulations as transcribed; a 2025 technical directive reduced the flap limit to 3 mm.
- The wing sits on two ridges, standing in for the car's endplate mounts.

**Planned:** three-point-bend coupons in six print orientations (ASTM D790 style) and tensile dogbones (ASTM D638 Type I); then the flap bench test with an FIA-style point load at 80% chord and a four-pad distributed load at the computed centre of pressure, read by dial gauges at both edges, against a ±15% acceptance gate. Later: a high-fidelity check (OpenFOAM, CalculiX and preCICE) and a carbon-fibre flap with bend-twist coupling.
